Task 1.1 Q3 ⬇️:
Quality correlates most with alcohol (r=0.48), volatile acidity (r=-0.40) and sulphates (r=0.25). Stronger, less vinegary wines tend to score higher.

The most correlated pairs are fixed acidity with citric acid and with density (both 0.67). The most anti-correlated are volatile acidity with citric acid (-0.55) and alcohol with density (-0.50). Residual sugar is almost uncorrelated with volatile acidity (0.00), sulphates (-0.01) and quality (0.01).

The dendrogram groups fixed acidity, citric acid and density together, so they overlap, but I would not call them redundant. Correlations of 0.5-0.7 are far from identical, and a weak feature may still help a model in combination with others.

Method: get_column_names() reads the labels and correlation_matrix() applies numpy.corrcoef to the load_data() array. cluster_order() turns 1 - r into distances and uses squareform and average linkage to put similar properties together. plot_heatmap() draws the reordered grid with dendrograms on two sides, a fixed -1 to +1 colour scale and each value printed. I tested each function and checked values against a separate calculation.

Further discussion: even the strongest link with quality is moderate, so no single property predicts quality. Pearson only measures straight-line relationships, and quality has few whole-number values (mostly 5 and 6), which keeps all correlations modest. A model combining all properties, as used in Tasks 2 and 3, is therefore a sensible next step.
